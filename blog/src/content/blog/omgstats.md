There is a cattle ranch in Australia that is roughly 23,677 km², bigger than the entire state of Israel (about 22,145 km²).

I read this stat twice when I came across it, checked the source, and made the noise I always make when a number breaks my brain.

I've always loved big stats like this. I couldn’t tell you why. Something about a single number reframing how you see the world in under a second. No essay, no argument, just a figure that quietly breaks your brain.

So I went looking for more. A few at first, then a few more. And somewhere around the tenth, I realised I was onto something interesting. It wasn't just that I liked them. They all gave me the same reaction, and that felt like it meant something.

As a designer, I couldn’t leave them in a notes file. That’s where good numbers go to die. Looking at that list, all I could think was that these were screaming to be tiny pieces of editorial design. The number is the headline. Everything else is context. Set the figure large, give it room to breathe, and suddenly it stops being a line of text you scroll past and becomes something you stop for.

They deserved to be set large. They deserved to be shareable.

Then came the domain. I discovered there was a great one still available: [omgstats.com](https://omgstats.com)

I’d half expected it to be taken, and it wasn’t. And the name does half the work, because it’s the reaction you have when you read one. It’s the noise, written down. You don’t need to explain what the site is for. The name tells you how you’re supposed to feel.

I bought it, built the site, and made one thing easy: share any stat, anywhere.

That was the whole idea, and it’s still the whole idea. Put a visual front end on some great data. Take something that’s already fascinating and make it look as good as it deserves to, in a form that travels. It sounds small, and it is, but it has since shaped most of what I build. Structured information in, something designed and shareable out. I keep finding new places that same approach works.

I’ve also fact-checked every stat on the site myself. That part isn’t optional. The entire thing runs on one promise, that the number you’re looking at is real, and fake numbers would sink it. A mind-blowing statistic that turns out to be wrong isn’t a mind-blowing statistic. It’s just a mistake with good typography.

There’s a lesson in here that I keep having to relearn: the best projects start with a reaction, not a business plan. I didn’t sit down to build a stats brand. I read a number, made a noise, and followed it. The plan came later, and it’s still forming.

Where it goes next, I’m not sure yet. But I want to do something with the brand beyond the screen, and a physical game is the idea I keep coming back to. A number, a pause, and that little jolt of “no way”, in your hands rather than on your phone.

For now, the collection keeps growing, and so does my stats obsession.
