How many of us are fixated on something to validate ourselves, like getting to $10K MRR from side projects?

It feels like the validation we need. The proof we’ve “made it” as such.

We start making decisions based on that goal instead of what actually interests us.

Kill project ideas because they don’t have an obvious path to revenue. Ignore the weird experiments because they don’t fit the playbook. Force ourselves to work on things that “should” succeed rather than things that excite us.

And weirdly? That’s when everything slows down.

What actually works
The projects that connect? Usually the ones we built because they interested us, not because they fit someone’s “road to $10K” framework.

The tools people actually use? We probably built them for ourselves first. They didn’t come from reverse engineering a revenue target.

The straitjacket problem
Here’s what keeps happening:

When we fixate on a specific outcome, we stop seeing the opportunities right in front of us.

We’re so busy trying to force one path that we miss the thing that would actually work, because it doesn’t look like what we expected.

The number becomes a straitjacket. Can’t move. Can’t pivot. Can’t follow the interesting idea because it doesn’t fit the plan.

Instead of asking “How do I hit $10K MRR?”, what if we asked:

”What work makes me feel energised and useful?”

When you follow genuine interest instead of a metric, things move differently.

Less forcing. More flow. Better work.

The results might not look like $10K MRR. They might look better. Or completely different. Or nothing at all, but at least we’ll have built something we actually cared about.

The honest thing here is that none of us know if this approach “works.”

But chasing the number clearly isn’t working either.

So maybe it’s worth trying: 
Build what interests us. See what happens. Stay open to opportunities we hadn’t imagined.

Get out of our own way and let the actual work guide us.

Worth a shot, anyway.
